Dungeon

A text-based dungeon RPG in C++, built around an object-oriented class hierarchy.

Overview

Dungeon is a terminal RPG in which a warrior explores ten connected rooms, fights monsters, trades with NPCs, and manages hunger, thirst, and poison while searching for the way out. Every game element derives from a single Object base class with a virtual triggerEvent(), so rooms can hold any mix of monsters, NPCs, items, and environments and run them polymorphically. Some rooms run a background timer thread that keeps draining the player's stats in real time.

Game Systems

Turn-based Combat

The player strikes first and picks a skill each turn. Skills cost MP, and three are unlocked through quests; monsters choose skills at random, and some lower the player's attack or defense or inflict poison. The player can flee, and the monster keeps its state for the next encounter.

Survival

Hunger and thirst rise by 2 with every action; at the cap of 50 the player starts losing health, and thirst also lowers attack. Food and water come from NPCs, monsters, and the lake.

Poison

Three poison types, each with its own damage per move and duration. Players can wait it out, find the milk, or trade captured animals for an antidote.

Real-time Environments

Desert, forest, and swamp start a background timer that raises hunger or thirst every 10 seconds until the room's event ends. In the swamp, the player must press space within two seconds to escape.

NPCs & Items

NPCs run lotteries, sell goods, and pose quiz questions. Items are one-time or permanent; permanent items go into the backpack and can be equipped during fights.

Save & Load

Character data is saved to file and restored, and character stats, the map, and ASCII art are loaded from files.

Challenges & Solutions

Circular includes between Player and Poison
Each header included the other, so the build failed. Moving the player.h include from poison.h into poison.cpp broke the cycle.
Draining stats in real time while waiting for input
Ran a timer in a std::thread that checks elapsed time with std::chrono and stops through a std::atomic<bool> flag once the room's event is done.
Robust terminal input
Validated almost every prompt and re-asked on invalid input, and used ANSI escape codes to color and bold key messages.
